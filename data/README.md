# Data

All data is synthetic. It is generated by `python -m src.generate_data` with a
fixed seed (`SEED = 42`) and a fixed "today" (`AS_OF_DATE = 2026-10-12`), both
defined in `src/config.py`. Running the generator twice produces identical files.

- `data/raw/` - generated files, including the injected data-quality issues.
- `data/processed/` - cleaned files, `closed_orders.csv` and `open_orders.csv`, written by `src/data_processing.py`.

## Raw datasets

| File | Rows | Content | SAP inspiration |
|---|---|---|---|
| `suppliers.csv` | 26 (25 suppliers + 1 duplicate) | Supplier master data | LFA1 |
| `purchase_orders.csv` | 508 (500 orders + 8 duplicates) | Orders placed from 2026-04-15 to 2026-10-10 | EKKO / EKPO |
| `deliveries_history.csv` | 1210 (1200 deliveries + 10 duplicates) | Closed deliveries ordered from 2025-01-01 to 2026-04-14 | EKBE (goods receipts) |
| `stock_levels.csv` | 20 | Stock, safety stock and daily consumption per material | MARD / MARC |

The table names are only an inspiration for the columns. No SAP system is involved.

## How the simulation works

- Each supplier has a hidden probability of delivering late. It is used by the
  generator only and is **not** written to any file.
- The probability of a late delivery increases for short promised lead times
  (14 days or less), unusually large quantities and electronics.
- Every order first receives a "true" delivery date. If that date is after
  `AS_OF_DATE`, the order is `Open` and its `actual_delivery_date` is blank.
  Six open orders are marked `Cancelled`.

## Late flag and supplier late rate

`delivery_delay_label = 1` when `actual_delivery_date` is more than
`LATE_TOLERANCE_DAYS` (default 0) days after `promised_delivery_date`, else 0.
It exists only for closed orders (`closed_orders.csv`: history + delivered POs).

For each open order, `supplier_late_rate` is the share of the supplier's closed
orders delivered before today that were late. Open orders have no late flag yet.
